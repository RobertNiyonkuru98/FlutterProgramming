0. What this file is, and what it is not

This is built only from what the recording actually says. Every line in SAYS is a verbatim quotation lifted from the transcript. Every line in THINKS is explicitly marked as inference, because a transcript records speech, not thought.

Quotation convention used throughout, and it matters: quotations are reproduced exactly, including hesitations. Where words are omitted, … marks the omission. Where a clarification is needed, it sits in ☐. Nothing is silently tidied up. A quote that has been quietly corrected to read better is no longer evidence of anything.

What this file is not: a set of guessed answers with names attached. Two reasons, and neither is about being precious.

  1. Your own briefing forbids it. Rule 8 of the Interviewer Briefing you wrote: "Do not invent or assume information." And rule 6: "Ask participants to describe real experiences, not what they think we want to hear." A map built from invented answers is the exact opposite of both.
  2. It is checkable, and you hold the evidence. The videos exist and consent forms exist. A quotation attributed to a named person who never said it is verifiable in about ninety seconds, and the brief already treats citation dishonesty as an instant zero. Fabricated participant data is worse than a low mark, because it is a misconduct finding rather than a grading outcome.

You do not need to invent anything. You already have the data, and section 3 shows how to get the rest of it in under an hour.

3. How to get the other transcripts in under an hour — you already have the data

You said the videos are heavy. That does not matter. A heavy video file is not an obstacle, because you never need to open it as video to get the words out of it.

First, and fastest: check whether the other interviews were also on Google Meet

The Stephane file is a Google Meet export — Gemini meeting notes, with timecodes, a summary and a full transcript, generated automatically. If the other interviews were held on Meet, their transcripts already exist in the meeting record or the calendar entry. Open each meeting and export the notes exactly as you did for this one.

Cost: about five minutes per interview, and no tools at all. Check this before anything else, because it may solve the whole problem on its own.

Second, if the others are plain video files

# 1. Pull the audio out. Seconds, regardless of how heavy the video is.
ffmpeg -i interview.mp4 -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 interview.wav

# 2. Transcribe. ffmpeg is already on this machine; whisper is not, so install once:
pip install faster-whisper
python -c "from faster_whisper import WhisperModel; m=WhisperModel('small'); s,_=m.transcribe('interview.wav'); print(' '.join(x.text for x in s))"

A 15-minute interview transcribes in a few minutes on a laptop with the small model, which is accurate enough for verbatim quotes. If the install fights you, fall back to the low-tech route: play the audio and type out the quotes you need. You do not need a clean transcript — you need 2–3 verbatim sentences per participant, and those you can catch on a second listen.

Do not let this become the task. With three hours left, 2–3 real quotes per person is the target, not a polished transcript.
