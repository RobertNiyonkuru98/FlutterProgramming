### P1 — Introduction + Literature review (Sun 13 Sep)
- **Introduction & Mission Alignment (3 pts)** — the rubric grades this separately and the submission list omits it. Write a short paragraph set: the community challenge, its social and economic significance, credible data, and how it aligns with your mission. *Cohesive, analytical, academically sound.*
- **Literature review**, exactly **5–7 credible sources**, **IEEE**, numbered `[1]…[n]` **in order of first appearance**.
- Build a **source table** first: *number → citation → the one statistic → page/quote → URL → date accessed*. Never cite a source you haven't read.
- Then **maximum 1 page, minimum 2 paragraphs, paragraphs only (no bullets)**: problem context → evidence with `[n]` → gap identification.
- Prefer sources that name Rwanda. "Pilot data from similar interventions in Kenya" is acceptable; nothing-country-at-all is not.

### P2 — Interview kit (Sun 13 – Mon 14 Sep)
Five artefacts, all in `02_Interview-Kit/`. **The rubric explicitly grades the recruitment plan, the recruitment email, and participant diversity — these are not optional extras.**
1. **Screener** (Google Form: age, gender, location, occupation + 1 qualifying question). ⚠️ **Recruiting only.** The brief rejects surveys as a research method — never present the screener as your research.
2. **Recruitment email/message** — WhatsApp-adapted, ≤100 words: who you are, why, ~25 minutes, no right/wrong answers, anonymous, no sales pitch. **Save a copy — it's graded.**
3. **Consent form** (the rubric wants an *ethical consent form*, not just a spoken line). One short page: purpose, voluntary, can stop anytime, anonymity, recording consent, contact. Read it aloud and **record the spoken "yes"**. Keep the signed/scanned copies — 5 points live here.
4. **Interview script — ONE script for everybody.** The brief: *the questions are the same, the answers differ.* **5 warm-up + 8–10 core questions**:
   - Open-ended, starting with **"why"**, **"what"**, **"how"**, **"walk me through…"**
   - **Zero yes/no questions.** ("Do you use X?" ✗ → "How do you handle X today?" ✓)
   - Cover: current behaviour, workarounds, frustrations, cost of the problem, what they've tried, what would make them switch.
   - **Pilot it** on one friendly non-target person, then cut anything that earns one-word answers.
5. **Logistics**: recording app tested on every member's phone, notebook + pen each, and a shared **interview calendar** so 12 bookings don't collide.
- **Participant diversity** is graded — deliberately mix at least two of: gender, age band, urban/peri-urban, income level, role in the group.

### P3 — Fieldwork (Mon 14 – Sat 19 Sep) — the only week requiring real effort
- **Target 12 interviews** (rubric: >10). Each member does 2, coordinator does the surplus.
- **Same week, same script, spread across 5 members.** That is what makes it light.
- Per interview, upload **the same day**:
  - `script-responses.pdf` — the script with that user's answers typed under each question
  - `audio.m4a` — consent obtained **on the recording**
  - `notes.jpg` — optional scanned handwritten notes
- **Capture quotes verbatim** — 2–3 striking sentences per person, in their own words. You need these for empathy maps, personas and the insights summary.
- **Capture observation, not just answers:** mood, hesitation, when they laughed, what they reached for. Graded under "rich evidence".
- **Bias discipline** (the brief names five biases — show you handled them): same questions for everyone; a diverse set, not 12 clones of yourselves; never describe your solution before asking about the problem; record everything so you don't remember only the first and last thing said; **don't pivot the problem statement because you already spent time on it** (sunk cost).

### P4 — Synthesis (Sun 20 Sep)
- **Individual empathy maps.** *Brief:* one per interviewed user. *Rubric:* "at least 3–4 key interviews".
  → **Do all 12 for brief compliance, but put the real detail — direct quotes *and* sensory notes — into 3–4 designated "key" maps.**
- **Sensory notes are a rubric requirement the brief never mentions:** each map's quadrants need **Sights, Sounds, Feels, Smells** alongside SAYS / THINKS / DOES / FEELS. *This is the single most commonly missed rubric item.*
- *Time-saver:* build one FigJam/Figma template with the quadrants, then duplicate it 12 times — ~5 minutes each instead of 20.
- **Aggregated empathy map(s)** — merge similar users into segments (e.g. *Positive*, *Negotiable*, *Confused*, or by role). 2–3 maps is realistic.
- **Personas — 2–3, "highly detailed".** Rubric requires **name, photo, demographics, goals, frustrations, behaviours, environment, and a quote** — the **photo** and **environment** are easy to forget. The quote must come from a real interview.
- **Insight summary**: number conducted, 5 strongest insights, each with a supporting quote and which participants said it. **Insight must be traceable to participants** (graded).

### P5 — Market & competitor analysis (Mon 21 – Tue 22 Sep)
- **Reviews 4–6 competitors** total, split direct (same offering + same audience) and indirect (similar offering/different audience, or different offering/same audience).
- For each: screenshot **every** key screen, note pricing, app-store rating, and — most valuable — **mine the 1-star and 3-star reviews for complaints**. That evidence is free and it becomes your differentiation.
- **User journey map for ONE direct competitor**: stages → actions → thoughts → feelings/emotional curve → pain points → **Opportunities**. The rubric is explicit: **"the 'Opportunities' column explicitly lists concrete, actionable improvements that map to your later Figma features."** Choose a direct competitor, not an indirect one.
- **UVP** — the rubric wants **a single, clear, compelling statement**: the key benefit, how it solves the user's need, and what makes it different.
- **Reframed problem** — restate the problem **from the user's perspective**, clearly, supported by deep empathy. This is a separate rubric requirement from the original problem statement; don't just repeat it.

### P6 — Design (Wed 23 – Sat 26 Sep)
1. **Information Architecture** — sitemap showing screen hierarchy. **Rubric: at least 8 screens.** Apply the brief's named principles: **Choice Principle** (few, well-organised options) and **Focused Navigation** (logical menu design).
2. **User flow diagrams — one per core task** (onboarding → complete first core action; plus one supporting task). Must show actions, decision points, **happy path AND key failure states** (wrong input, no network, empty state, payment failure).
3. **Storyboard — 6–10 panels**, with **characters, setting and emotions**, telling one user's story from problem → using your app → outcome.
4. **Figma, wireframe → high-fidelity:**
   - **Design tokens + component library first** (type scale, colour tokens, icons, reusable components). The rubric names "design tokens" — do this before building screens and they get 3× faster.
   - **60-30-10 colour rule** (60 dominant / 30 secondary / 10 accent) — named in the brief, so it's marked.
   - **10+ screens**, covering **the complete primary user flow up to a success state**, plus Home, Profile, Settings and your unique feature.
   - **Navigation, transitions, form fields, hover/tap states and empty states must be realised** — not implied (rubric).
   - **Interactive**: real clickable links, real transitions, at least one simulated state change.
   - **"Screens must explicitly map to the empathy map, journey map and UVP."** → Add a short caption under each key screenshot naming which insight or journey pain point it answers. Costs 20 minutes, defends several points at once.
   - **Share setting: "Anyone with the link can view."** Test in an incognito window in P6, not on submission day. A link that requests access = zero.

> **Format strategy:** narrative sections in paragraphs; everything else as images. That satisfies "no bullets" without losing content.

**Two hard safety gates before submitting:**

**Gate 1 — IEEE cross-check (mismatch = automatic zero).**
Every in-text `[n]` must have exactly one matching reference, in the right order. **Two people check independently** — one reads every in-text number aloud, the other confirms against the reference list. Sign off in the tracker.

**Gate 2 — Originality / AI check (>50% AI-flagged = automatic zero).**
- **Write the proposal yourselves**, in your own voice, from your own interview notes.
- AI **only** to fix grammar on text you *already wrote* — exactly what the brief allows. The rubric phrases it as ">50% AI-generated content (limited to brainstorming/editing assistance)", and *brainstorming* and *editing assistance* are the permitted lanes.
- Keep Google Docs **version history** as evidence the drafts came from you.
- Tell-tale to avoid: uniform sentence rhythm across every section, and citation-shaped claims with no raw interview quote behind them. Your interview quotes are the part an AI can't have written — they're also your best defence.
