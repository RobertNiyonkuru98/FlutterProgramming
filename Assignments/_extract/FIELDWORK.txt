1. Problem Statement (academic style)

Newcomers to Rwanda -- including international students, expatriate professionals,
and other recent arrivals -- frequently carry health insurance issued by a foreign or
unfamiliar provider. While the majority of pharmacies in Rwanda accept cash
payment regardless of insurance affiliation, a subset of pharmacies additionally
accept specific insurers, and this information is only made available through a
physical notice -- typically a board or sticker -- displayed at the pharmacy's
entrance. Consequently, this information, although factually public, is not accessible
in any centralized, searchable, or remote form.

In practice, an individual seeking to determine whether a nearby pharmacy accepts
their insurance has only two available methods: physically travelling to the pharmacy
to read the posted notice, or contacting the insurance provider directly to request the
information verbally. Both methods are time-inefficient, and neither scales to
situations involving multiple pharmacies, unfamiliar neighbourhoods, or
time-sensitive circumstances such as a medical emergency. This inefficiency is
compounded for newcomers, who additionally lack local knowledge of pharmacy
locations, operating hours, and emergency contact procedures.

This gap -- between information that already exists publicly and the absence of any
digital means to access it remotely -- constitutes the central problem this project
addresses. The proposed intervention does not seek to alter existing healthcare or
insurance procedures, but rather to digitize an existing, verifiable source of public
information (the pharmacy-door notice) into a searchable, location-aware mobile
application, thereby reducing the time, uncertainty, and physical effort currently
required to locate insurance-compatible healthcare providers.

2. Steps and Algorithm -- How the App Performs in Use

User-facing steps (standard flow)

    1. Onboarding -- user opens the app and selects their insurance provider from
         a list (or enters "no insurance / cash only").

    2. Location access -- app requests the user's current location (or a manually
         entered area).

    3. Provider retrieval -- app queries its database for pharmacies and hospitals
         within a defined radius that are tagged as accepting the selected insurer.

    4. Filtering & sorting -- results are filtered by distance and "open now" status,
         and sorted nearest-first.

    5. Results display -- user sees a list/map of matching providers, each showing
         name, distance, open/closed status, and accepted insurers.
    6. Provider detail view -- user taps a result to see full address, hours, phone
         number, directions, and "what to bring" (e.g., insurance card, prescription, ID).

    7. Navigation handoff -- user taps "Directions" and is routed via a maps app or
         in-app routing.

    8. Emergency mode (parallel path) -- at any point, a persistent one-tap
         Emergency button lets the user skip all filtering and immediately see the
         nearest open provider plus the correct emergency number, with an option to
         call directly.

Based on your problem statement, algorithm, pharmacist research, and the
rubric, the design instructions should make the IA, user flows, and storyboard tell
one consistent story. The diagrams should reflect the actual problem you are
solving: helping newcomers remotely identify healthcare providers that match
their insurance, availability, and location.
