# 2. Get four interviews — the 35-minute version

### Send this now, to individuals, one at a time

> Hey [name] 👋 Quick favour — 15 minutes today, on a call. We're doing our final project on how people who've moved to Rwanda find a pharmacy that takes their insurance. You qualify if you arrived in the last two years. No right answers, anonymous, nothing sold. **Free at 17:00 or 17:45?** I'll call you, 15 minutes.

### Then the group version

> Hi all 👋 We're ALU students doing our final project on a real problem: when you arrive in Rwanda and need medicine, there's no way to know which pharmacy takes your insurance without walking there and reading the sign on the door. **We need 15-minute phone calls today**, anyone who arrived in Rwanda in the last 2 years. Anonymous, no sales pitch. Reply with a time. 🙏

### Three accelerators

- **Ask your 2 existing participants for one referral each.** *"Who else arrived recently?"* Referrals convert faster than any group post, and they widen the sample without extra work.
- **A "yes" is not an interview. A time is.** Reply with two options: *"17:00 or 17:45?"*
- **The pharmacy run is optional, with a rule.** At 14:00, if you have **fewer than 2 slots confirmed**, go to a pharmacy — one in-person interview with a pharmacist buys you the diversity segment, the only real sensory notes you will get, and a photo of the board that is evidence for your whole problem statement. If you already have 3+ slots, skip it and spend the hour on Figma. Call ahead if you can; most Kigali pharmacies trade on Sundays but hours vary, **and a pharmacy closed at 15:00 on a Sunday is itself a finding worth writing down.**

### The 15-minute script

1. **Screener:** When did you arrive in Rwanda, and which insurance do you hold?
2. Walk me through the last time you needed medicine here. What happened, step by step?
3. How did you decide which pharmacy to go to?
4. How did you find out whether they take your insurance?
5. What did you do when you weren't sure?
6. How long did that take, and how many trips did it cost you? *(push for a number)*
7. What if it were late at night, or a weekend? What would you do?
8. What would have made that easier?

**Never describe the app before question 8.** The moment you mention it, the rest of the answers are contaminated and useless for an empathy map.

**Per interview, capture or it did not happen:** consent spoken on the recording · the recording · 2–3 **verbatim** quotes · what you saw, heard and smelt · uploaded to `03_Interviews/` **that hour**.

# 3. Figma, alone, in 3 hours — the method that works

**Do not design 10 screens. Assemble 10 screens.**

| Step | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 min | **Colour + type tokens.** One brand colour, one accent for the primary action, mostly light neutrals. Name them. |
| 2 | 40 min | **Components:** button (default/pressed), input field, list row, card, bottom nav, header, status chip, icon set. |
| 3 | 2 h | **The 10 screens.** Start at the highest level of the flow and go down. |
| 4 | 20 min | **Interactions + states:** link the primary flow end to end, plus empty state (no results), loading, and a tap state. |
| 5 | 10 min | **Share → anyone with the link → open it in an incognito window.** |

**The 10 screens** (this covers the whole journey and satisfies the row):

1. Onboarding / value statement · 2. Select your insurer · 3. Location permission · 4. **Results — pharmacies accepting your insurer** · 5. Pharmacy detail (open now, what to bring, directions) · 6. Search / filter by insurer · 7. **Emergency mode** · 8. Home · 9. Profile & insurance card · 10. Settings + saved pharmacies

**Make them simple and consistent, not beautiful.** Ten consistent simple screens score the same as ten pretty ones, and they take a third of the time. The rubric asks for **navigation, transitions, form fields, tokens, hover/tap and empty states**, and a **public link** — it does not ask for visual flourish.

**Then 15 minutes that defends several rows at once:** put a one-line caption under each key screenshot naming the research finding it answers. The rubric wants screens that map explicitly to the empathy map, journey map and UVP, and captions are the cheapest way to prove it.

# 4. The other diagrams, fast

**Competitor analysis (3 pts, 45 min).** Four competitors: two direct (a tool that helps someone find a provider accepting their insurance, international is fine), two indirect (Google Maps, an insurer's app or the RSSB portal, and WhatsApp word of mouth). Per competitor: features, strengths, weaknesses, the gap it leaves us, URL, and **proof you opened it**. Then twenty minutes on the **1★ and 3★ app-store reviews** — those complaints are your differentiation evidence, in a competitor's own users' words.

**IA + user flows (6 pts, 40 min).** The IA is a sitemap of the 10 screens you just listed, with hierarchy and the two principles named in the brief (Choice Principle, Focused Navigation). The flows need the **happy path and the failure states**: wrong insurer selected, no results, no network, pharmacy closed. Do this one at a time and it is quick.

**Journey map (6 pts, 45 min).** Pick **one direct competitor**, actually use it, and map stages → actions → thoughts → feelings (draw the emotional curve) → pain points → **Opportunities**. Every opportunity must **name one of your 10 screens**:

> ✗ *"Better experience."*
> ✓ *"Show accepted insurers on the results card so the user need not open each pharmacy — screen 04."*
